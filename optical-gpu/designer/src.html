Scaling with core size N (same platform, model)

Light-written weights hold their value at zero power, so efficiency keeps improving as N grows: converter energy per MAC falls as 1/N. Thermo-optic weights grow as N², which caps silicon-photonics meshes. Die length grows as N, which is the main cost of weak index contrast.

    What this is, honestly
    
        
    An "optical GPU" here means an array of analog optical tensor cores. Each one computes y = W·x for an N×N matrix in a single pass of light, at the modulator symbol rate. Control flow, memory, nonlinear activations and accumulation stay in an electronic host (FPGA or ASIC). That is how every photonic AI accelerator is built. The precision is about 4–6 effective bits, which suits inference workloads that tolerate low precision.
    
        
    The method
    
        
      
          
    • Matrix → mesh. W = U·Σ·Vᵀ (SVD). Vᵀ and U are unitary and are factored into a rectangular mesh of N(N−1)/2 MZIs each (Clements decomposition). Σ is a column of MZI attenuators. When singular values repeat, the free rotation in the SVD is randomized, so no switch sits at an exact bar or cross state.
      • 
          
    • Device physics. A mode solver and split-step BPM design the cell (single-mode width, 50:50 coupler, bends at the bend-loss limit). The process is centered on written test MZIs. Several noisy writes give the pool of real devices: coupler angles, arm-phase offset and loss.
      • 
          
    • Calibration. After writing, each MZI is measured. Its phases are re-solved for its real couplers, with leftover phase errors carried forward and absorbed by later shifters and the receiver phase. The weight patches are then rewritten closed-loop. Without calibration, no mesh computes.
      • 
          
    • Layout → plate. Waveguides are written at half the material's index range, and weight patches use the other half (patch length λ/Δn gives 2π). The die is cut into exposure tiles at a slab pixel of w/4. A DMD images each tile directly, or a phase SLM or printed plate projects it holographically (Gerchberg–Saxton with a lens term = Fresnel plate).
      • 
        
    
        
    Not modelled
    
        
    • Vertical confinement: the slab is treated as 2-D with an effective index.
    • Thermal crosstalk between patches during writing.
    • Crossings: none are needed.
    • Wavelength dependence of the couplers, which limits WDM channel count.
    • Packaging tolerances.
    • Sb₂Se₃ multi-level stability over time.
